# Directorial Taste: observable aesthetic decisions, not hidden model thought

## Taste engine: discriminate before animating

For every proposed moment ask:

1. **Why now?** The move follows a narrative beat, user action or information change.
2. **What wins attention?** One clear focal hierarchy: primary / supporting / ambient.
3. **What is withheld?** Negative space, silence and offscreen continuity can be more powerful than filling the frame.
4. **What survives?** A dot, line, card, photo, graph point or viewpoint can carry the audience through cuts.
5. **What is its physics?** Choose controlled inertial, crisp editorial, soft mechanical or organic — don't mix contradictory easing.
6. **Can it be read on a phone?** The most beautiful 6K animation fails if its 9:16 type is unreadable.
7. **Can the film work muted?** The hook, information and payoff must survive a silent first viewing.
8. **What could be removed?** Every decorative effect needs to earn its screen time.

## House aesthetic = a *starting point*, not a universal ban

- Preferred: one signature visual idea; legible sans-serif; precise masks; strong contrast; quiet depth; clean type tracking; weighted compositions; fast setups / decisive reveals / shorter rests.
- Often reject: default centered-on-gradient title, fake product screens, shockwave rings, heavy RGB split, gratuitous neon and glow, jitter, arbitrary particles, inflated feature claims, generic HUD chrome and 3D objects without purpose.
- Allow deliberately: gradients, 3D, particles or intense motion **only** when conceptually relevant and coherent with product and references.

## Three distinct visual directions

| Direction | Composition | Motion | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| restrained-editorial | asymmetrical type, generous whitespace, thin rules | cut-on-idea, masks, eased object handoffs | premium brand, trustworthy utility |
| kinetic-product | true UI detail and gesture, close crop, common object | FLIP/shared elements, beat-matched state changes | functional demos, quick app reels |
| spatial-cinematic | layered world and depth, deliberate hero angle | one camera vector per move, parallax, physical settle | product reveals, spatial experience |

Choose **one dominant direction**, borrow from others sparingly. Never imply one aesthetic is universally superior.

## Two-pass critique

- **Pass A (blind visual):** score pacing, silhouette, readability, continuity, distinction, visual noise from the rendered frames, without seeing its prompt.
- **Pass B (on-brief):** compare actual film to authentic assets, product facts, intended audience, story objective and sound cues.
- A film that follows the prompt but looks mediocre is mediocre. Authority comes from the delivered film, not the sophistication of wording.

## Observable research takeaway

The Playbook's blind re-grade found that longer prompts did not reliably make more visually impressive films, while structured briefs bought product fidelity and control. Accordingly, use constraints to improve *precision* and critique to improve *taste*. Generate direction alternatives when the visual thesis is weak. Source: https://github.com/PHY041/claude-motion-playbook/blob/main/docs/prompt-patterns.md


## Opus 5.5 public-gallery-informed refinements (2026-10-17)

- Start with an **identity chain**: describe which visible element survives between shots; reject arbitrary transitions without a story reason.
- Use the **poster blindness test**: static covers cannot establish animated quality; require timed frames for judgments of timing and spatial continuity.
- Add a **replication-gap critique**: when a creator's original and Skillry remake share a prompt, compare corresponding decoded film beats to learn missing controls (layout, motion physics, asset fidelity, rhythm); do not presume the same text creates the same film.
- Distinguish **style signatures** (e.g. quiet bright UI vs tactile illustrated narrative vs type-led dark product launch). Reject the idea that all Opus 5.5 films have one visual aesthetic.
- For controlled motion, a physical interaction must begin where the user gesture occurs and finish with visually legible resulting state.
- For each film's intended shot sequence, specify the **narrative reason** for holding/cutting and the **visual proof** that the feature exists; compositional polish cannot replace reality.

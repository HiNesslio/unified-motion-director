# Creative direction and narrative system

## Promise / audience / transformation

Complete: "After seeing this, [audience] should understand [one truth] and feel [change], because the film shows [credible proof]."

For 6–15 s: cold open 0–20%, one or two proof beats 20–75%, brand/payoff 75–100%.
For 15–45 s: hook → tension/context → working proof → release/payoff → end identity.
For a 45–90 s film: chapters can expand but still require a single thread and meaningful escalation.
These are starting distributions, not requirements or claims based on research.

## Creative routes (always genuinely different)

- Route 1: **Object protagonist** — a small real UI element persists through multiple jobs; useful for connected features.
- Route 2: **One-take camera world** — movement through product states builds a sense of discovery.
- Route 3: **Editorial contrasts** — pattern/scale contrasts and confident cut placement keep hierarchy sharp.

For each route provide tagline, central metaphor, five-beat arc, signature reveal, biggest risk, and why it fits product goals.

## Scene existence test

If removing a shot would not damage understanding, emotion, or rhythmic punctuation, remove or combine it. Give each shot an explicit `purpose` and `why_this_scene` (not a vague "looks good").

## Copy and truth

- Use supplied strings exactly when representing shipped UI or official brand wording.
- Claims require sources; label concept UI as "concept" in working materials, never silently portray it as shipping.
- Placeholder 12:34, 10k users, 99.9% are fake metrics — avoid unless transparently mock data.
- On-screen text should usually be short (especially vertically). Check Chinese line breaks and text fitting after font loads.

## Director's treatment format

1. **Logline:** One sentence, not feature list.
2. **Creative thesis:** Metaphor and reason.
3. **Emotional progression:** Start feel → end feel.
4. **Shot motif:** Persistent object / camera / cut logic.
5. **LOOK:** exact layout, type, palette, contrast, and exclusions.
6. **Sound:** real track provenance, sync direction, no-voice policy where specified.
7. **Proof assets:** what actual screenshots, logo, footage and UI text are supplied.
8. **Success and risk:** what viewers understand in 5 seconds; risks to legibility and truth.
